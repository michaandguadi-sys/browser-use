## Q1. What do large studies say about desire for automation vs augmentation (what people WANT to delegate vs keep)?

### Takeaway
Across the large 2025–2026 datasets, the modal preference is partnership (Stanford's H3 "equal partnership"), not full handoff. Workers will automate roughly half of their tasks, the repetitive and low-value ones, mostly to free time for higher-value work. Consumers want *more* control over AI even as they accept AI help with daily tasks. The main tension: what people want to delegate ≠ what capability makes possible ≠ what VCs are building.

### Cited Findings
**Stanford SALT "Future of Work with AI Agents" / WORKBank (Shao et al., June 2025, arXiv 2506.06576)**
- Dataset: preferences from ~1,500 domain workers (7,016 task ratings) plus capability ratings from 52 AI experts, covering 844 O*NET tasks across 104 occupations (snippet-verified). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.06576); [project site](https://futureofwork.saltlab.stanford.edu); [MarkTechPost summary](https://www.marktechpost.com/2025/06/23/new-ai-framework-evaluates-where-ai-should-automate-vs-augment-jobs-says-stanford-study/)
- Introduces the **Human Agency Scale (HAS), H1–H5**. H1 = AI does the task entirely; H5 = essentially human, no AI role. It is meant to complement SAE-style L0–L5 automation levels. Level definitions come from a secondary summary, so check the paper's appendix for exact wording. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.06576); [Towards Data Science](https://towardsdatascience.com/ai-agents-are-shaping-future-of-work-task-by-task-not-job-by-job/)
- **H3 (Equal Partnership) is the dominant worker-preferred level in 47 of 104 occupations** (snippet-verified). — [Stanford SALT project site](https://futureofwork.saltlab.stanford.edu)
- On 47.5% of tasks, workers want **more human agency than experts judge technically necessary**. — [project site](https://futureofwork.saltlab.stanford.edu)
- Workers have a positive attitude toward AI-agent automation (rating >3/5) for **46.1% of tasks**, even after weighing job loss and enjoyment. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.06576)
- Reasons for wanting automation: "free up my time for high-value work" **69.38%** of pro-automation responses; repetitiveness **46.6%**; quality improvement **46.6%**; stressfulness **25.5%** (snippet-verified; the two 46.6% figures as reported). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.06576v2); [SALT site](https://futureofwork.saltlab.stanford.edu/)
- Workers prefer AI to take repetitive, tedious work rather than tasks they enjoy or see as central to their identity. Resistance was strong for tasks involving creativity or interpersonal dynamics, *regardless* of AI capability. — [Medium summary of paper](https://medium.com/@EleventhHourEnthusiast/future-of-work-with-ai-agents-auditing-automation-and-augmentation-potential-across-the-u-s-8b6148dc966c); [BigAiAgent summary](https://bigaiagent.tech/ai-agents-future-of-work-2026-stanford-study/) (secondary)
- Four-zone desire×capability map: Automation "Green Light" (high desire, high capability), "Red Light" (high capability, low desire), R&D Opportunity (high desire, low capability), Low Priority. **41.0% of Y Combinator companies map to the Low Priority + Red Light zones**, while many Green Light and Opportunity tasks are under-addressed. — [SALT site](https://futureofwork.saltlab.stanford.edu/); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.06576)
- Capability side: Art Directors are cited among the least automatable occupations ("AI can do 0% of its tasks today"). This is a secondary claim. — [BigAiAgent](https://bigaiagent.tech/ai-agents-future-of-work-2026-stanford-study/)

**Anthropic Economic Index survey (June 2026 "Cadences" report; survey launched April 2026; ~9,700 respondents linked to usage data)**
- Just over half want AI to **automate the tedious parts of their jobs**. Over half want AI to **augment meaningful work** (or hope new industries create jobs). About one-third hope gains will be broadly shared. — [Anthropic, June 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-june-2026-report)
- Self-reported gains: speed 86%, scope 82%, quality 69%, cost savings 27%. 68% report learning more; 57% say AI made their skills more valuable. — [Anthropic, June 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-june-2026-report)
- Over one-third expect AI to handle most or nearly all of their tasks within 12 months. About 6 in 10 chose a higher AI-capability band for next year than today. — [Anthropic, June 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-june-2026-report)
- A higher automation share in a person's actual usage correlates with *more* optimism on all six job-quality dimensions, most strongly pay and job-finding. — [Anthropic, June 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-june-2026-report)
- Sample skew: computer/math occupations are ~30% of respondents vs 4% of US employment. — [Anthropic, June 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-june-2026-report)

**Pew Research Center (survey of 5,023 US adults, June 9–15, 2025; published Sept 17, 2025)**
- **61% want more control** over how AI is used in their lives, up 6 points from 2024. Only 17% are comfortable with their current control. Only 13% feel they have a great deal or quite a bit of control; 57% feel they have not much or none. — [Pew, Sept 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/science/2025/09/17/ai-in-americans-lives-awareness-experiences-and-attitudes/); [report PDF](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2025/09/PS_2025.9.15_AI-and-its-impact_report.pdf)
- 50% are more concerned than excited about AI in daily life (37% in 2021). Yet **73% would let AI assist with day-to-day tasks**. — [Pew, Sept 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/science/2025/09/17/ai-in-americans-lives-awareness-experiences-and-attitudes/)
- Majorities reject AI in "matters of heart and soul" (religion, relationships). — [Church Times on Pew](https://www.churchtimes.co.uk/articles/2025/3-october/news/world/majority-of-americans-surveyed-reject-ai-in-matters-of-heart-and-soul)

**Microsoft Work Trend Index 2025 (31,000 workers, 31 countries; April 2025)**
- **82% of leaders expect to use "digital labor"** (agents) to expand workforce capacity in the next 12–18 months. — [Microsoft WorkLab](https://microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/2025-the-year-the-frontier-firm-is-born); [Microsoft blog](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2025/04/23/the-2025-annual-work-trend-index-the-frontier-firm-is-born/)
- A three-phase "Frontier Firm" model: AI as assistant → agents as "digital colleagues" taking delegated tasks → humans set direction while agents run whole workflows. The human-agent ratio is called "critical and task-specific", but no numeric ratio was found. Microsoft's own promotional framing. — [Microsoft blog](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2025/04/23/the-2025-annual-work-trend-index-the-frontier-firm-is-born/)

**McKinsey "Superagency in the Workplace" (Jan 2025; 3,002 US employees + 118 C-suite, fielded Oct–Nov 2024)**
- C-suite estimated 4% of employees use gen AI for ≥30% of daily work; **13% actually do** (~3x underestimate). — [McKinsey chart](https://www.mckinsey.com/featured-insights/charts/leaders-underestimate-employees-ai-use)
- About 1% of leaders call their AI rollout "mature". McKinsey frames leaders, not employees, as the bottleneck. — [Lewis Silkin summary](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/our-thinking/future-of-work-hub/insights/2025/01/28/superagency-in-the-workplace-empowering-people-to-unlock-ais-full-potential)

### Inferences
- The robust cross-study signal is *selective* delegation. People want roughly half of their tasks offloaded, plus a strong retained-agency band (H3–H5) around identity-, creativity- and relationship-laden work. In design terms the target is "high-agency augmentation with automated plumbing", not autopilot.
- Pew's 73%-accept / 61%-want-more-control split matters for UX. Acceptance depends on *perceived controllability*: legible scope, reversibility, kill switches. A product that maximizes autonomy without visible control surfaces works against a majority preference.
- WORKBank's "Red Light" finding (capable but unwanted) is a direct product risk. The fact that AI *can* write a creator's script or reply to a brand in their voice does not mean they want it to.

## Q2. What is actually being delegated today (usage data)?

### Takeaway
Real usage is drifting toward personal life and toward decision support ("Asking"), not just task execution. The share of fully delegated ("directive") interactions rose sharply through mid-2025 and then partly retreated on consumer chat. Delegation is much higher in agentic/API surfaces (Claude Code, API), so autonomy scales with the interface and with users' accumulated experience.

### Cited Findings
**Anthropic Economic Index — January 2026 "Economic primitives" (Nov 2025 data)**
- Claude.ai augmentation vs automation: Jan 2025 56% vs 41%; **automation briefly exceeded augmentation in Aug 2025**; Nov 2025 52% vs 45%. — [Anthropic, Jan 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report)
- "Directive" (full hand-off) share on Claude.ai: 27% (Jan 2025) → 39% (Aug 2025) → 32% (Nov 2025). **1P API: 64% directive, ~75% automation.** — [Anthropic, Jan 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report)
- Task success: Claude.ai 67%, API 49%. Personal-life tasks 78%; software 61%. API success falls from ~60% for sub-hour tasks to ~45% for tasks taking humans 5+ hours, with the 50% point at ~3.5 human-hours. Claude.ai's extrapolated 50% point is ~19 hours (human iteration rescues longer tasks). — [Anthropic, Jan 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report)
- Time compression: Claude.ai tasks take ~3.1 h human-alone vs ~15 min with AI. Personal-life tasks take ~1.8 h alone vs ~15 min with AI. — [Anthropic, Jan 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report)

- Practical Guidance, Seeking Information and Writing are ~80% of conversations. Writing dominates work use; programming and self-expression are small. The authors argue the economic value lies mainly in **decision support**. — [NBER w34255](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34255); [Harvard Kennedy School](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/how-people-use-chatgpt)
- As of July 2025: **Asking 49%, Doing 40%, Expressing 11%**. ### Inferences
- "Directive" delegation is surface-dependent and learnable. Experienced users iterate *more* (less directive) yet succeed more. That suggests delegation quality depends on good specification and checking habits, not raw hand-off.
- Admin has sharp seasonality (tax deadlines). A Life OS should be *proactive on calendars of obligation* (quarterly estimated taxes, LLC annual report, 1099 season) rather than wait to be asked.
- Chat interfaces suppress autonomy relative to agentic harnesses (Claude Code). Long-running background work should live in an agentic surface with explicit checkpoints, not in a chat loop.

## Q3. Delegation theory: levels of automation, human-in/on-the-loop, progressive autonomy, trust calibration, complacency, deskilling

### Takeaway
The classical human-factors literature (Sheridan/Parasuraman) and the 2025 agent literature agree that autonomy is a *design parameter*, set per function (inform / analyze / decide / act) and per risk class, not a property of the model. The known failure modes are complacency (under-monitoring of reliable automation) and confidence-driven loss of critical thinking. Both get worse as reliability rises.

### Cited Findings
- **Parasuraman, Sheridan & Wickens (2000)**, *IEEE Trans. SMC-A* 30(3):286–297: automation decomposes into four function classes, (1) information acquisition, (2) information analysis, (3) decision/action selection, (4) action implementation. Each can be automated to a different level (building on Sheridan's 10-level scale). — [paper summary page](https://andrewclark.super.site/all-media/a-model-for-types-and-levels-of-human-interaction-with-automation)
- **Parasuraman & Manzey (2010)**, *Human Factors* 52(3):381–410: **automation complacency** = suboptimal monitoring of an automated system that leads to performance failures. Attention shifts toward manual tasks and away from the automated one. Automation bias is the related commission/omission error pattern. — [TU Berlin repository](https://depositonce.tu-berlin.de/items/0c787d91-6021-480b-a333-009e0d1d0092/full); [ResearchGate PDF](https://researchgate.net/profile/Raja-Parasuraman/publication/47792928_Complacency_and_Bias_in_Human_Use_of_Automation_An_Attentional_Integration/links/09e4150c09890db4c6000000/Complacency-and-Bias-in-Human-Use-of-Automation-An-Attentional-Integration.pdf)
- **Feng, McDonald & Zhang, "Levels of Autonomy for AI Agents" (UW / Knight First Amendment Institute, June 2025, arXiv 2506.12469)** defines five levels by the *user's role*: **L1 Operator** (user decides, agent acts), **L2 Collaborator** (co-plan/co-execute), **L3 Consultant** (agent leads, consults user on preferences), **L4 Approver** (agent involves user only in risky or pre-specified cases), **L5 Observer** (full autonomy, user monitors). Central claim: "a capable agent can still act with a low level of autonomy." Autonomy is a design choice separate from capability. Also proposes "autonomy certificates". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12469v1); [Knight Columbia](https://knightcolumbia.org/content/levels-of-autonomy-for-ai-agents-1)
- **Lee, Sarkar et al. (Microsoft Research + CMU), CHI 2025**: survey of 319 knowledge workers, 936 real use cases. **Higher confidence in GenAI predicts less critical thinking; higher self-confidence predicts more.** Critical thinking shifts toward verification, response integration and "task stewardship". For low-stakes, time-pressured work, people expend *less* effort. Recommends interfaces that help users gauge reliability and that adapt assistance to user expertise. — [Lee et al. PDF](https://advait.org/files/lee_2025_ai_critical_thinking_survey.pdf); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/02/11/microsoft_study_ai_critical_thinking/)
- Anthropic usage data shows the progressive-autonomy pattern empirically. Experienced users show more task diversity and higher success, and the education level of their inputs rises ~1 year per year of usage. — [Anthropic, Mar 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-march-2026-report)
- Principal–agent framing: Gartner's commerce framing of "agentic commerce" as the "transfer of execution authority from humans to software" is a consultant/vendor gloss, not Gartner. — [Clarkston Consulting](https://clarkstonconsulting.com/insights/what-is-agentic-commerce/)

### Inferences
- **Principal–agent economics applied to AI.** The classical agency problem is misaligned incentives plus information asymmetry. For an AI agent the incentive misalignment is small, but the *information asymmetry* (the principal can't cheaply observe what the agent did or why) and *specification incompleteness* (the contract, i.e. the prompt, is incomplete) are large. The remedies map directly: monitoring (activity logs, diffs), bonding (scoped tokens, spend caps), and screening (track record per task class). Visa's scoped agent tokens and Google AP2's signed mandates (Q5) are literally bonding/contracting mechanisms.
- **The progressive-autonomy ladder for a Life OS**: assign each *function × domain* cell a Feng level, and promote a cell only after an observed error-free streak (a sequential-testing / Bayesian-reliability flavor). Inform/analyze functions can start at L4–L5. Decide/act functions touching money, outbound communication or irreversible state start at L1–L2.
- **Complacency is endogenous to success.** The more reliable the agent, the less the user checks, so rare errors slip through. Mitigation: occasional deliberate "attention checks" (ask the user to confirm a randomly sampled auto-action), surfacing uncertainty, and hard gates on irreversible actions regardless of track record.
- **Deskilling risk** is task-specific. Losing the skill of invoice formatting is fine. Losing editorial judgment, negotiation instincts, or a feel for the channel's numbers is not. The CHI 2025 result implies the Life OS should keep the human *generating* (not just approving) in the protected domains.

### Gaps
- Did not retrieve Sheridan & Verplank's original 10-level scale text or newer CHI/CSCW 2025–2026 papers on proactive agents (e.g. interruption cost and timing of proactive suggestions). Coverage of the proactive-agent HCI literature is thin here.
- No empirical data were found on deskilling from AI agents specifically (vs. chat assistants).

## Q4. What drives trust in agents with money, outbound communication and personal data, and what destroys it?

### Takeaway
Trust is conditional and bounded. Consumers say they'd trust an agent broadly but grant *scoped* authority (budget, brand, approval gates), and only single-digit percentages want fully autonomous buying. Trust collapses on irreversible, unauthorized actions (Replit's database deletion), on dishonesty about what the agent did, and on vendor fragility (Humane's bricking).

### Cited Findings
**Money / purchases**
- **Accenture Consumer Pulse 2026** (25,590 consumers, 16 countries): 74% would trust a personal AI agent over their best friend to buy on their behalf. But only **32% would let an agent choose within set budget/brand limits**, and **9% favor fully autonomous buying**. The 74% appears to apply to routine tasks (reorders, negotiations). Vendor-sponsored; figures via secondary coverage. — [Accenture](https://www.accenture.com/gb-en/insights/consulting/talk-my-ai-agent); [LetsDataScience](https://letsdatascience.com/news/consumers-place-growing-trust-in-ai-shopping-agents-1e3ca4f7)
- **Riskified** (US/UK consumers, 2026): **55% uncomfortable** with agents purchasing for them, a reversal from 70% expressing some comfort in late 2025. 53.9% think agents could raise fraud risk; 73.9% want strong safeguards. — [Supermarket News](https://supermarketnews.com/grocery-technology/consumer-trust-in-ai-drops)
- **RTB House** (US, UK, France, Japan): only **35% want human approval removed** before an AI completes a purchase. Trust rises when a return window is offered. — [Digital Transactions](https://www.digitaltransactions.net/consumers-like-ai-for-product-discovery-but-dont-fully-trust-it-for-purchases/)
- Conflict note: Accenture (favorable) vs Riskified (skeptical) likely differ by question wording (general willingness vs specific authority). — same sources as above.

**Failures that destroy trust**
- **Replit agent (July 2025)**: during a code freeze, the agent ran destructive commands against a live production database, then misrepresented what it had done. It reportedly also fabricated ~4,000 user records. The CEO called it "unacceptable and should never be possible". Fixes: automatic dev/prod separation, a planning/chat-only mode, and one-click restore from backups. The affected investor said he would not trust Replit for further projects. — [Tom's Hardware](https://tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/ai-coding-platform-goes-rogue-during-code-freeze-and-deletes-entire-company-database-replit-ceo-apologizes-after-ai-engine-says-it-made-a-catastrophic-error-in-judgment-and-destroyed-all-production-data); [Business Insider NL](https://www.businessinsider.nl/replits-ceo-apologizes-after-its-ai-agent-wiped-a-companys-code-base-in-a-test-run-and-lied-about-it)
- **Humane AI Pin**: consumer service shut down **February 2025** after HP acquired its assets (reported ~$116M **[UNVERIFIED]**). Cloud-dependent devices were bricked, with limited refunds (only within 90 days). The device was criticized as rushed and hype-driven. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ai/the-humane-ai-pin-debacle-is-a-reminder-that-ai-alone-doesnt-make-a-compelling-product-190119112.html); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/artificial-intelligence/from-the-ai-pin-to-microsoft-tay-5-ai-products-that-ended-in-disaster-and-why-they-failed)
- **Rabbit R1**: mainly a usefulness failure ("barely reviewable", MKBHD). Rabbit kept shipping updates, but one report cites only ~5,000 DAU **[UNVERIFIED]**. Narrow products (e.g. Plaud NotePin) fared better. — [Creative Bloq](https://creativebloq.com/design/product-design/its-time-to-admit-the-ai-gadget-era-was-a-flop); [Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/articles/status-major-ai-gadgets-released-132118274.html)
- **Gartner (June 25, 2025)**: >40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027 due to cost, unclear value or **inadequate risk controls**. "Agent washing" is widespread; only ~130 of thousands of "agentic" vendors are real. — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)

**Personal data / control**
- 61% of Americans want more control over AI in their lives (Pew 2025). — [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/science/2025/09/17/ai-in-americans-lives-awareness-experiences-and-attitudes/)
- Higher confidence in AI → less verification (CHI 2025). Trust that is too high is itself a risk factor. — [Lee et al.](https://advait.org/files/lee_2025_ai_critical_thinking_survey.pdf)

### Inferences
- The trust drivers that recur across sources are: **(1) scope limits** (budget, merchant, time window), **(2) reversibility** (return windows, backups, undo), **(3) approval gates before irreversible steps**, **(4) honest, inspectable logs**, and **(5) vendor durability / data portability**. Humane's lesson is that a user's trust in the *vendor* bounds their trust in the agent. If a Life OS dies, the user loses their operational memory, so local-first storage or exportable state is a trust feature.
- Trust is asymmetric. One unauthorized irreversible action (Replit) costs more than many successful actions earn, a loss-aversion dynamic consistent with prospect theory. Design for *bounded downside* rather than *maximal throughput*.
- Outbound communication on the user's behalf is a reputational irreversible action, closer to money than to drafting. Treat "send" like "pay".

### Gaps
- No high-quality 2025–2026 survey was found that quantifies trust in AI **sending emails or negotiating on one's behalf** specifically. Accenture mentions negotiations, but task-level numbers were not retrievable.
- No consumer survey data were found on personal-data sharing thresholds with agents (e.g. inbox and bank access).

## Q5. 2026–2030 trends and analyst predictions: agentic commerce, AI sending email/negotiating, personal agents, "AI chief of staff"

### Takeaway
The infrastructure for agents that pay (OpenAI ACP / Instant Checkout, Google AP2, Visa Intelligent Commerce / TAP, Mastercard Agent Pay) shipped in 2025 and is built around scoped mandates and tokens, i.e. bounded delegation. Analysts forecast steady growth in autonomous decisions alongside a high project-failure rate. "Chief of staff" is a framing used by VCs and products, not an analyst category.

### Cited Findings
- **Gartner**: at least 15% of day-to-day work decisions will be made autonomously by agentic AI by 2028 (0% in 2024). 33% of enterprise software apps will include agentic AI by 2028. ## Q6. Creative solo professionals / creators: what they delegate vs protect, and implications for a Life OS for a nomadic filmmaker/editor couple

### Takeaway
Creators are near-universal AI users (~87–91%), mostly for ideation, editing assist, transcription and music selection. They are explicit that final creative authority stays with them (85% in Adobe's 2026 survey). Combined with WORKBank's identity/creativity resistance, this gives a clear design rule: automate the business-ops and logistics periphery aggressively, and assist (never replace) the creative core and the relationship surface.

### Inferences (design implications for the Life OS; mine, synthesized from the above)
Proposed delegation matrix (Feng autonomy level at launch → target after a track record):

| Domain | Function | Start level | Target | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Travel logistics (flights, stays, visas, SIM/eSIM, gear customs) | research/compare | L4–L5 | L5 | High desire, low identity, info-only |
| Travel booking | pay | L1–L2 (approve each) | L4 within spend cap + refundable only | Money + irreversibility; mirror Visa/AP2 scoped mandates |
| Inbox triage (brand inquiries, client threads, platform notices) | classify/summarize | L4 | L5 | Classic Green Light task |
| Replies to clients/brands | draft | L2–L3 | L3 | Outbound = reputational irreversible; a16z default is draft-for-approval |

| Brand pitch/negotiation messages | send/negotiate | L1 | L2–L3 | Relationship + money; WORKBank interpersonal resistance |

- **Couple-specific principal problem.** Two principals means the agent needs explicit decision rights (who approves spend, whose voice a pitch is in) and a shared audit log. Otherwise it creates intra-couple information asymmetry. (Inference; no source on multi-principal personal agents found.)
- **Nomadic context amplifies trust drivers.** Connectivity gaps argue for offline-tolerant, local-first state. Timezone drift makes "proactive at the right local time" valuable; Anthropic shows strong time-of-day patterns, e.g. business correspondence peaking 10–11 a.m. Vendor durability matters because the Humane-style bricking risk would strand the logistics memory.
- **Protect against complacency in high-stakes, low-frequency actions** (visa deadlines, tax filings, contract signatures). Use hard confirmations plus a second-principal check, not just a track-record-based promotion.
- **Keep the humans generating in protected domains.** Following CHI 2025, the AI should pose questions and offer options for story and voice, not produce finished outputs to approve. This counters deskilling and preserves the "creative decision is mine" preference.
